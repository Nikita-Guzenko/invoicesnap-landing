Estimate vs invoice vs quote

Use an estimate to give the expected price before the work. Use an invoice to ask for payment after the work. Use a quote when you offer a fixed price.

Side by side

EstimateQuote or bidInvoice
Send itBefore the workBefore the workAfter the work or a stage
Use it toGive an expected priceOffer a price for a defined scopeAsk for payment
If the price can changeSay so in the wordingSay the price is fixed, or say what changes itIssue a corrected invoice

What binds you

Check the rule of your state. For example, New Jersey asks that home improvement contracts over $500, and all changes to them, be in writing. So write what you mean: "this price can change if…" or "fixed price for the scope above".

A simple order of work

  1. Send an estimate with scope and exclusions.
  2. Get a signature, and a contract if your state asks for one.
  3. Write a change order for anything new.
  4. Send an invoice per stage or at the end.

What each paper should say

On the estimate: the scope, what is not included, the date until the price holds, the deposit, and how the price can change. On the invoice: the number, the date of the work, each line, what the customer already paid, the balance and the due date.

Do not reuse the estimate number on the invoice. Keep the estimate number in the notes, so that both papers can be matched later.

Which word to use with a customer

Use "estimate" when hidden conditions can move the price, such as old pipe behind a wall. Use "fixed price" or "quote" when you have seen all the work and will hold the number. If a customer asks for a "bid", say in one sentence which of these your paper is.

Short answers

Can I turn an estimate into an invoice?

Yes. Copy the lines into the invoice form, add changes, and give the invoice its own number and date.

Is a signed estimate a contract?

Ask a local lawyer before you rely on it.
