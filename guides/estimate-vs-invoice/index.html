Estimate vs invoice vs quote

An estimate gives the expected price before the work, and an invoice asks for payment after the work. A quote is a word that people often use for a fixed price.

Side by side

EstimateQuote or bidInvoice
WhenBefore the workBefore the workAfter the work or a stage
PurposeGive an expected priceOffer a price for a defined scopeAsk for payment
Can the price change?Yes, if the wording allows itOften no, unless the scope changesOnly by a corrected invoice
Main partsScope, exclusions, valid-until dateScope, price, termsNumber, date, amount, due date

The words have no single legal meaning

The US has no federal definition. The wording of your paper and your state law decide what binds you. For example, New Jersey asks that home improvement contracts over $500, and all changes to them, be in writing. So write what you mean: "this price can change if…" or "fixed price for the scope above".

A simple order of work

  1. Send an estimate with scope and exclusions.
  2. Get a signature, and a contract if your state asks for one.
  3. Write a change order for anything new.
  4. Send an invoice per stage or at the end.

What each paper should say

On the estimate: the scope, what is not included, the date until the price holds, the deposit, and how the price can change. On the invoice: the number, the date of the work, each line, what the customer already paid, the balance and the due date.

Do not reuse the estimate number on the invoice. Keep the estimate number in the notes, so that both papers can be matched later.

Which word to use with a customer

Use "estimate" when hidden conditions can move the price, such as old pipe behind a wall. Use "fixed price" or "quote" when you have seen all the work and will hold the number. Some customers ask for a "bid", which usually answers their own written scope. Say in one sentence which of these your paper is, because the customer will read the word as a promise.

Short answers

Can I turn an estimate into an invoice?

Yes. Copy the lines into the invoice form, add changes, and give the invoice its own number and date.

Is a signed estimate a contract?

It can be, depending on its terms and your state. Ask a local lawyer if the job is large.
