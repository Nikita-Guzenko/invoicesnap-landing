What to include on an invoice

A US invoice should show who is billing, who pays, a unique number, the date, what you sold, the amount with any tax, the due date and how to pay.

The field list

US federal law has no list of required invoice fields. The list below is common practice. The IRS names invoices among the supporting documents for your books, which is why a clear number and date matter.

FieldExampleReason
Your name, address, contactRivera Tile LLC, 10 Grout StThe payer must know who bills.
Customer name and addressAnn Cole, 5 Pine RdIt shows who owes the money.
Invoice number1047Each number is unique, so you can find it later.
Invoice date and service date11/08/2026, 11/06/2026It places the income in time.
Description, quantity, priceTile labor, 6 hours, $70The customer can check each line.
Subtotal, tax, total$420, $0, $420Sales tax rules come from the state.
Due date, payment methodNet 14, checkIt removes the first question that a customer asks.

Fields that some jobs add

  • A license number, where a state asks. Contractor and electrical pages show examples.
  • A PO number for large clients.
  • A rights line for writing and creative work.
  • A deposit paid and a balance due.

How long to keep it

The IRS says to keep records for 3 years in most cases, and longer in some named cases. Keep a copy of each invoice.

Invoice or receipt

An invoice asks for money that is due. A receipt shows money that was paid. If the customer pays on the spot, one paper can do both when you print the amount paid and a zero balance. The form has a field for what the customer already paid.

Mistakes that slow payment

  • Two invoices with the same number. Your books and the customer records then disagree.
  • "Services" as the only description. A finance team cannot match that to an order.
  • No due date. The customer then decides when to pay.
  • A sales tax line with no rate. The customer cannot check the amount.
  • A business name that differs from the name on your Form W-9.

Short answers

Does an invoice need a signature?

No US federal rule asks for one. Your contract or your customer can ask for one.

Does an invoice need a tax ID?

No federal rule asks for one on the invoice. A business client may ask for a Form W-9 instead.
