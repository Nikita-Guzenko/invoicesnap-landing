What to include on an invoice

Put these on each invoice: who is billing, who pays, a unique number, the date, what you sold, the amount with any tax, the due date and how to pay.

The field list

The list below is common practice, not a legal list. The IRS names invoices among the supporting documents for your books, which is why a clear number and date matter.

FieldExampleReason
Your name, address, contactRivera Tile LLC, 10 Grout StThe payer must know who bills.
Customer name and addressAnn Cole, 5 Pine RdIt shows who owes the money.
Invoice number1047Each number is unique, so you can find it later.
Invoice date and service date11/08/2026, 11/06/2026It places the income in time.
Description, quantity, priceTile labor, 6 hours, $70The customer can check each line.
Subtotal, tax, total$420, $0, $420Show the sales tax line apart.

Fields that some jobs add

  • A license number, if you need one. Contractor and electrical pages show examples.
  • A PO number if the client asks for one.
  • A rights line for writing and creative work.
  • A deposit paid and a balance due.

How long to keep it

The IRS says to keep records for 3 years, unless one of its named longer periods applies. Keep a copy of each invoice.

Invoice or receipt

Use an invoice to ask for money that is due. Use a receipt to show money that was paid. If the customer pays on the spot, print the amount paid and a zero balance. The form has a field for what the customer already paid.

Mistakes that slow payment

  • Two invoices with the same number. Give each invoice its own number.
  • "Services" as the only description. Name the work instead.
  • No due date. Write a date.
  • A sales tax line with no rate. The customer cannot check the amount.
  • A business name that differs from the name on your Form W-9.

Short answers

Does an invoice need a signature?

Your contract or your customer can ask for one. Check your contract.

Does an invoice need a tax ID?

A client that files an information return on you asks for your taxpayer number on Form W-9.
