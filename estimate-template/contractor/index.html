What a contractor estimate should say

A remodel estimate has to be specific enough that two contractors could price the same work. It names the scope, allowances, exclusions, schedule and payment steps.

FieldWhy it matters on a remodel
Scope by trade or roomThe customer sees what the number buys.
AllowancesSay the amount and who pays when the choice costs more.
ExclusionsList what a homeowner might assume, such as appliances or hidden damage.
ScheduleA start date and a duration.
Payment scheduleTie payments to stages. Check any deposit limit in your state.
License and insuranceThe FTC tells customers to choose licensed and insured contractors, so print the number.

Estimate, quote or bid: Say what your paper means. Write "fixed price", or say how the price can change. Ask your state board or a lawyer what binds you. See estimate vs invoice.

Rules to check before you send a contractor estimate

A signed estimate may become the contract

New Jersey requires a written contract for home improvement sales over $500, and all changes must also be in writing. Check whether your signed estimate meets the contract rules of your state. N.J.A.C. 13:45A-16.2

Cancel period at the home

A buyer may cancel a sale made at the home until midnight of the third business day after the sale, under the FTC rule. It has limits, so read it if you sign at the kitchen table. 16 CFR 429.1

Deposit limit in California

The down payment on a home improvement job there is capped at the lesser of $1,000 or 10 percent, except for blanket-bond licensees. The sample deposit is only an example. CSLB guide

Contractor estimate questions

What is a good contingency line?

It depends on your scope and the age of the home. Show it on its own line and say how you will use it. Bring unused amounts back to the customer.

Should I show my markup?

Choose what your contract needs. If you bill cost plus a fee, put the fee on its own line. On a fixed price, give one number per stage.

How do I handle allowances?

Enter a line with the unit set to allowance. In the terms, say that the customer pays the difference when the choice costs more, and gets a credit when it costs less.

Do I need a separate contract as well?

It depends on your state. New Jersey, for example, requires a written contract for home improvement sales over $500. This form does not add contract terms. Ask a local lawyer.
