What a landscaping estimate should say

A landscaping estimate states the area, the materials by name, what you haul away and whether the plants carry a warranty. The customer can then match bids.

FieldWhy it matters in a yard
Area measuredSquare feet or cubic yards let the customer compare two bids.
Plant and material namesSod type, mulch type and size of each plant.
Prep workSay what grading, stripping and soil cost.
Haul-awaySay if debris leaves the site.
Watering and careSay what watering you expect for any plant warranty.
Tax treatmentTexas taxes landscaping labor, so state the tax line where your state asks for it.

Estimate, quote or bid: Say what your paper means. Write "fixed price", or say how the price can change. Ask your state board or a lawyer what binds you. See estimate vs invoice.

Rules to check before you send a landscaping estimate

Texas sales tax

Texas lists planting, mowing, trimming and edging grass, and tree care, as taxable. Add state and local rate, unless you work alone with yearly receipts of $5,000 or less. Texas Comptroller 94-112

Pesticide license in Texas

A person who applies any pesticide to ornamental plants, trees, lawns or weeds for hire needs a Texas Department of Agriculture license. A person who applies only fertilizer does not. Texas Department of Agriculture

California C-27 class

The C-27 landscape contractor constructs, maintains, repairs and installs landscape systems and facilities. Print the number on the estimate when you hold it. CSLB C-27

Landscaping estimate questions

Should I price a mowing contract as one estimate?

Yes. Enter visits as the quantity and the visit price as the unit price. The total shows the full season.

How do I handle soil that is worse than expected?

Exclude it, or give a unit price per cubic yard for added soil. Then you can bill it without a dispute.

Do I need a separate line for delivery?

Add one when the material price does not include it.

Can I ask for a deposit on a landscape job?

Enter the percent on the form. Check the rule of your state first.
