What an electrical estimate should say

A clear electrical estimate names the load, the panel plan and the permit, and it says what happens if the existing panel cannot carry the new load.

FieldWhy it matters before the job
Load calculation resultIt decides whether the price holds.
Panel work in or outA panel upgrade is the largest swing in a charger or addition job.
Who supplies the hardwareName who buys the charger, fixtures or fan.
Permit and inspectionSay who pulls the permit and whether its fee is included.
Open wall workPatch and paint are often excluded. Write that.
Valid untilCopper and breaker prices move, so set a date.

Estimate, quote or bid: US law gives these words no single meaning. Your wording and your state decide what binds you. Write "fixed price", or say how the price can change. See estimate vs invoice.

Rules to check before you send an electrical estimate

Florida: number on each offer

Florida Statutes 489.521 says that the registration or certification number must be stated in each offer of services, business proposal or advertisement. Check the section for your license type. Florida Statutes 489.521

Permit first in Washington

Washington L&I says that electrical work requires a permit and inspection, with a few exceptions, so add the fee to the estimate. Washington L&I

Who does the work in Texas

Texas requires an electrical license under Chapter 1305. Name the licensed person or company on the estimate. Texas TDLR

Electrical estimate questions

Should I quote a panel upgrade inside a charger estimate?

Give it as a separate option. Then the customer can accept the charger, and the upgrade only if the load calculation needs it.

Do customers need separate pricing for parts?

Not always. A lot price is simple. Show parts apart when your state taxes them apart from labor, and use the tax choice on the form.

How much deposit should I ask for?

It depends on the state and your material cost. California limits the down payment on home improvement jobs. Check your state before you enter the percent.

What is the difference between an electrical bid and an estimate?

A bid usually answers a request for a price on a defined scope. An estimate is your priced view of the job. Write the scope either way, and say if the price is fixed.
