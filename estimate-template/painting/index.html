What a painting estimate should say

A paint estimate says which surfaces, how many coats, how much prep and which paint. Most price gaps between two painters come from prep, so write it down.

FieldWhy it matters on a paint job
Surfaces and roomsWalls, ceilings, trim and doors are separate decisions.
Coats and paint gradeName the coats and the product line, or who buys the paint.
Prep workPatching, sanding, caulk and primer. Say how much is included.
Home ageA home from before 1978 can bring the EPA lead rule.
Moving and protectionSay who moves furniture and how you cover floors.
Color changesA new color after the start can need another coat.

Estimate, quote or bid: US law gives these words no single meaning. Your wording and your state decide what binds you. Write "fixed price", or say how the price can change. See estimate vs invoice.

Rules to check before you send a painting estimate

Lead-safe work in older homes

Under the EPA rule, paid work that disturbs painted surfaces in homes, childcare facilities and preschools built before 1978 falls under the Renovation, Repair and Painting Program. Lead-safe certified firms do the work. Name your certification on the estimate. US EPA RRP

Trained staff

EPA says that workers must be trained, either as a certified renovator or on the job by a certified renovator. Keep the training record with the job file. US EPA RRP

State license class

California has a C-33 Painting and Decorating class that covers preparing a surface and applying paints and similar materials. Other states set their own limits. Print the number your state gave you. CSLB C-33

Painting estimate questions

Should I price by the room or by the square foot?

Rooms are easy for customers. Square feet suit exterior work and big interiors. The form has a unit for each line, so use both on one estimate if you want.

Do I include paint in the price?

Say so either way. Add paint as a parts line if you buy it, or write in the exclusions that the owner buys it.

How do I handle damage found under old wallpaper?

Exclude it in the scope and set a unit price for skim coat or repair, so that no one argues later.

Should the estimate give a finish date?

Give a planned start and a duration in the scope. Weather can move exterior work, so say that in the terms.
