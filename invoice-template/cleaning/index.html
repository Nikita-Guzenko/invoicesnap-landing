What a cleaning invoice must show

A cleaning invoice lists the visit dates and what each one covered. No federal form exists, but dates and add-ons answer most client questions, and the IRS treats the invoice as a record of income.

FieldWhy it matters on a cleaning job
Visit datesOn a recurring plan, one invoice covers several dates. List them.
Service typeStandard, deep and move-out cleans have different prices.
Home or office sizeIt explains the price.
Add-onsOven, fridge and windows get their own lines.
Sales tax lineSome states tax cleaning and others do not.
Cancel or lockout policyPrint it, so that a lockout fee is no surprise.

Cleaning rules that touch the invoice

Tax on cleaning in Texas

The Texas Comptroller says tax is due on cleaning any building. A self-employed housekeeper employed by the household is exempt. Enter the combined rate on the form. Texas Comptroller 94-111

Collect, then remit

Texas also says that maid, janitorial and custodial operators should generally collect sales and use tax. Other states differ, so ask your own revenue office. Texas Comptroller 94-111

Certification not required

BLS says that janitors and building cleaners typically need no formal education and no certification. Print a bond or insurance line only if you hold one. BLS

Cleaning invoice questions

Do I send one invoice per visit or one per month?

Either works. A monthly invoice with the dates in the notes means less paper for a recurring client.

How do I bill tips?

Tips are not part of your price. Leave them off the invoice unless the client asks you to add one.

Should I charge when a client locks me out?

If your policy says so and the client saw it. Add a lockout line, and keep the message that shows that you came.

Do I need the square footage on the invoice?

Only if you price by it. For a visit price, write the bedrooms and bathrooms instead.
